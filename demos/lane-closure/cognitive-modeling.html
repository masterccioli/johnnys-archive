A demonstration, using the lane-closure simulation

Cognitive Modeling

Behaviour is what we can see. Cognition is what we hypothesise is going on inside the black box between the environment and the behaviour. A cognitive model makes that hypothesis explicit enough to run.

What a cognitive model is

At a lane closure we can observe behaviour: the open lane crawls, a few drivers fly down the closing lane and merge at the cones, most get over early and wait. We can also observe the environment: the cones, the queue, the car alongside. What we cannot observe is the processing in between. We could try to relate environment to behaviour directly, but the same environment produces early mergers, line cutters and pace holders, so the cause of the behaviour is not the environment alone. A cognitive model hypothesises the processing: a small set of internal processes, each with a setting, through which the environment is turned into behaviour. Behaviour is then the result of the environment and the cognitive process together.

Two things make such a model good, and they pull against each other. A simple model needs few processes, few settings, to produce the behaviour. A robust model reproduces the behaviour that is actually observed in the target population, across the whole pattern and not just one corner of it. Any behaviour can be reproduced by a model with enough dials, and any model is simple if it is allowed to fit nothing. The goal of cognitive modeling is the balance: the simplest hypothesis about cognition that is still robust to what people do.

1 · The observed pattern

The target. In a real study this would come from human drivers; here it is a fixed reference pattern that stands in for data, so the exercise is about the method. The model below is scored against it.

Error per measure is |model − observed| in units of a typical spread (100 s of wait, 10 places, 20 % share, 2 mph). Fit = mean error across measures; 0 is perfect.

2 · Hypothesised processes

Each checkbox is a mechanism the model is allowed to use. Unchecked, it is held at a neutral value for everyone, so it can't explain anything. Dashed boxes are extra hypotheses; they start off. Fewer checked boxes is a simpler model; a lower fit error is a more robust one. The starting model doesn't fit the observed pattern: try adding one extra mechanism at a time and watch the fit error. Some buy robustness; most only cost simplicity.

3 · Driver types and their settings

Columns are driver types, the top row is how much of the population each one is. Every other row is a process; it only appears when its mechanism is checked. Values are the type's mean; individuals scatter around it.

How this would work with real data

With human drivers you would record the same measures: waits by group, who gains places on whom, lane speeds. Each candidate model is run many times, its summary pattern compared to the data with a likelihood or a distance like the one here, and the parameters fitted by search rather than by hand. Then models are compared on two axes at once: how well they reproduce the data, and how many free parameters they needed to do it. Information criteria (AIC, BIC) turn that into a single number; cross-validation asks whether the fit survives on data the model never saw. A model that needs a new dial for every new observation has explained nothing; one that reproduces early mergers, line cutters and pace holders by moving a single setting has found a plausible piece of cognition. Simplicity and robustness are both required, and the model that balances them is the better account of the mind behind the behaviour.

  • Processes here are driver parameters: merge pressure (base, distSens, dScale), speed relative to the open lane (zip), gap acceptance, yielding, lane presence (pStay), give-up (queueSens), re-engagement, noticing distance (horizon), shorter-lane choice (laneSense), social contagion (normSens), a pace-matching gadget (gadget), and personal car-following (T, s0).
  • Road physics are shared and not part of the hypothesis: 34 mph free speed, a 5 mph crawl past the cones, arrivals above capacity, an entry queue of 3 per lane.
  • The reference pattern is the average of three long runs of one particular configuration of this same simulator (all mechanisms on); it is a stand-in for data, not data. The starting model is deliberately missing some of what generated it.
